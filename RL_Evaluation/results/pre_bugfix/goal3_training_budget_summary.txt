[Small vs. large dashboard, qualitative comparison] Both dashboards converge and plateau well within the 100-episode budget, but the large 15-visualisation dashboard shows a materially different (and more informative) convergence profile: (1) it converges slightly later (~episode 14-20 vs. ~episode 10 for the small dashboard); (2) episodes 8-10 came close to the minimum 10%-success-rate floor (21, 21, and 23 valid actions out of a required >20, vs. a minimum of 65 ever seen on the small dashboard) - episode 9 alone needed 3,502 invalid (masked-out) sampling attempts before collecting enough valid ones, indicating the pre-convergence policy on a larger/more varied dashboard can be transiently much less sample-efficient; (3) post-convergence, the large-dashboard policy becomes fully deterministic (200/200 valid actions, 0 invalid attempts every episode from ~14 onward), whereas the small-dashboard policy remains mildly stochastic even after reward plateaus (65-175 valid actions per episode throughout). This suggests 100 episodes is sufficient for both dashboard sizes to reach a stable policy, but the margin of safety around the 10% validity floor shrinks as dashboard complexity grows, and the near-miss on the large dashboard's episodes 8-10 is a specific point worth flagging for Discussion/Limitations rather than assuming the floor is comfortably cleared for any dashboard size.

[100-episode baseline vs. 500-episode stretch, direct comparison] R_Final at episode 100 was 0.3978 in the baseline run; R_Final at episode 500 in the stretch run is also 0.3978 (identical to 4 decimal places), and every episode from ~14 through 500 sits at that same value. Reward does not continue improving past the original 100-episode cutoff - the model had already converged well before episode 100, and extending training 5x further produces no further gain. This directly supports the claim that 100 episodes was sufficient (in fact substantially more than necessary) for this reward surface; it does not on its own establish sufficiency for larger/more complex dashboards (see the small-vs-large comparison above), where convergence is later and less consistently margin-safe against the 10% validity floor.
